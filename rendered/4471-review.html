Parser reads day-first dates as month-first

The shared parser always reads month first. In a day-first tenant, an ambiguous date such as 07/04/2026, intended as 7 April, becomes 4 July. App and email reports use this path. Form reports from tenants on booking-v2 also belong here because its DD/MM/YYYY hint agrees with their locale.

R-41200 from Harbour Dental gives the 7 April to 4 July example. R-41205 is another parser report, even though intake guessed hint from an en-US browser. Its tenant, Morrow Tax, uses fr-FR and booking-v2.

The required parser behavior is to preserve the tenant's date convention or accept an explicit unambiguous representation. The acceptance checks proposed by this investigation cover round trips for ambiguous day-first dates across app, email, and booking-v2 inputs. Include a month-first control and a day above 12 to catch fallback behavior. The export contains the desk's classifier, not the production parser, so these are proposed acceptance checks rather than a shipped patch.

Booking-v3 prints the wrong hint for day-first tenants

booking-v3 prints MM/DD/YYYY, but the form reads dates in the tenant's day-first order. A person follows the hint and types 01/06/2026 for 6 January. The form reads 1 June. R-41230 reports that result at Copper Studio. Its browser is en-GB and intake guessed parser, but the tenant runs booking-v3, so this is a hint report.

This problem affects form submissions from Alder Physio, Copper Studio, and Pale Harbour. App and email reports from those same tenants still belong to the parser group. While booking-v3 was in service, the hint and the form's interpretation needed to agree with the tenant locale. The proposed acceptance checks required a person following the displayed hint to get the intended date on booking-v3, with booking-v2 and month-first controls. A parser-only fix would not correct the form's misleading hint. The desk chose to retire this form rather than fix it.

Proposed decision: merge only a confirmed shared cause

Replace the subject-only instructions on the triage page with this workflow after desk review.

  1. Count each report ID once. Read the body and look up the tenant, its locale, its form version, and the channel.
  2. Use causeOf and compare with the existing ticket's cause. Merge confirmed matching causes even when their subjects differ.
  3. Keep different causes apart even when subjects match. Give each cause its own engineering ticket and count.
  4. If the tenant or cause is unknown, keep the report separate for investigation. Two unknown causes are not a confirmed match.
  5. Keep conflicting symptoms in the ticket as follow-up evidence. Reclassify only when the investigation supports it.
